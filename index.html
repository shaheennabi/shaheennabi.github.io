About

Currently building an “open post-training system” and an “open distributed training system” to train and advance open-weight models through reinforcement learning on long-horizon coding and agentic tasks.

Focused on building the complete RL stack: distributed orchestration, rollout generation, sandboxed environments, verifiers, training, inference, and evaluation. Building reliable, scalable infrastructure with robust APIs, automated testing, and observability, while studying training dynamics, debugging failures, and running controlled experiments to improve model capabilities over increasingly long horizons.

Optimizing end-to-end training and inference performance through distributed systems engineering, GPU and TPU kernel development, memory and communication optimization, profiling, and benchmarking. Focused on eliminating bottlenecks, maximizing hardware utilization, and improving throughput and efficiency across accelerator architectures.

Previously, I worked as a researcher at Carnegie Mellon University on long-horizon agentic systems, studying where models fail across extended coding and agentic tasks and developing evaluation environments and verifiers to identify failures in context retention, state tracking, planning, and execution. Before that, at the Indian Institute of Science (IISc), I studied the internal representations associated with reasoning and memorization in language models.

My research spans the full stack around reasoning models — post-training methods that build reasoning (RLVR, preference optimization, reasoning distillation), the test-time strategies that make it efficient (compute allocation, deciding when to stop deliberating), and the empirical and evaluation work needed to trust any of it. I have been going deeper into mechanistic interpretability — studying the internal circuits and representations behind multi-step reasoning in large language models, to understand failure modes like reward hacking and unfaithful chain-of-thought well enough to actually fix them, not just detect them. A specific thread: disentangling memorization from genuine reasoning mechanistically — which circuits are doing lookup versus computation — and using that understanding to work toward guiding reasoning safely, so models can be steered away from unfaithful or unsafe reasoning traces rather than just penalized after the fact.

My work brings together reinforcement learning, agent environments and evaluation, distributed systems, and accelerator performance. I share research code, experiments, and technical explanations through GitHub and Substack.
